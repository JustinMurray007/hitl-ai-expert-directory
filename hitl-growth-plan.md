# Human in the Loop Directory: First Growth Plan (draft v1)

Labels: **[Nevo]** means the point comes from Nevo's Brain, with the source named. **[Mine]** means it's my own reasoning. No metrics or results are assumed for this product.

---

## 1. Which side to start with: supply (AI specialists) first, recruited one by one with demand in mind

- **[Mine]** Nevo's Brain has no passage on which side of a marketplace to build first, so this call is mine. A directory that founders visit and find empty loses their trust. A specialist, on the other hand, can get value from a free listing (a profile page, a backlink, credibility) before any buyers show up. Recruit a small, hand-checked set of specialists in one niche (see section 2) first, then bring in founders.
- **[Nevo]** This follows the playbook Nevo used to start Gitroom. He built **Git Library**, a free site where open-source founders could list their projects and get discovered by influencers. Then he emailed his target audience with a humble pitch: "I don't have anything to sell you... everything is free." Nearly every email became a newsletter subscriber, and he kept nurturing them with weekly or monthly content. His framework: "give, give, give, give, then take in private." *(Source: "Outbound-to-Inbound Cold Outreach for Open-Source Developers", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3p97bZzBHx8; "Getting Around 10 Newsletter Subscribers a Day with Lead Magnets", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wQmI7zZn1g)*
- **[Mine]** Don't build supply in isolation, though. Make "real human accountability" a vetting standard that specialists earn (for example: named owner, response-time commitment, references). The badge is what makes the listing worth having, and it's the promise founders are paying for.
- **[Mine]** Manually match the first founders. Doing things that don't scale on the demand side shows you what founders actually ask for. That's how you'll learn whether "which AI should I use" or "who can implement it" is the real hook.

## 2. Sharper niche and positioning

- **[Nevo]** Niche down by **audience** without shrinking the total market too far. His example: "a CMS for doctors is more relevant than a generic CMS when a doctor is searching." *(Source: "How to Choose a Startup Idea in 2026", https://x.com/wickedguro/status/2090778859352563921)*
- **[Nevo]** Serve an audience that incumbents don't serve, and don't compete on being cheapest. *(Same source; also Nevo's X posts, Jun 2026, https://x.com/wickedguro)*
- **[Nevo]** An unknown company needs a plain-words headline that states the problem and the solution. Vague slogans are for mature brands. Test the message by paying people on Upwork or UserTesting to explain back what you do. *(Source: "Writing a clear website headline for a developer product", https://gitroom.com/blog/perfect-company-messaging)*
- **[Nevo]** The narrower and more specific the niche, the easier it is to get cited by ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity (AEO). *(Source: "Distribution Playbook for Growing Postiz from $2M to $3M ARR", https://x.com/wickedguro/status/2086788813301661865)*
- **[Mine]** Suggested angle: own "accountable AI implementation for non-technical small and medium business owners," and lead with one or two verticals Justin knows (e.g., agencies, e-commerce, clinics, or professional services). Generic "AI expert" marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal-style) sell talent. Your wedge is **accountability plus advice on what to integrate**, so say it outright.
- **[Mine]** The name: "Human in the Loop Directory" is clear to technical people but may not land with non-technical owners, and "Directory" undersells the guidance piece. Keep it as a working name and test it against the taglines below with the paid-tester method above.

**Tagline options [Mine]:**
1. "Find out which AI your business needs, and hire a vetted expert who answers for the result."
2. "AI implementation experts with a real human accountable for every project."
3. "Not sure what AI to add? Get a plan and a vetted, accountable specialist to build it."

## 3. Top 3 channels to start with, and the first actions for each

### A. Founder-led outreach to specialists (supply)
- **[Nevo]** Outreach makes sense when you have no audience yet and your target group is specific. Offer free value and never "sell by selling." Messages that offer to hire people or bring them something get replies, while sales pitches get ignored. Look beyond email and LinkedIn to Slack, Discord, Skool, and Circle communities. *(Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3p97bZzBHx8; "Agent-first positioning... (Alex is Building interview)", https://x.com/AlexIsBuilding/status/2079192005003919602)*
- **[Nevo]** Use a separate, warmed-up domain for cold email so it doesn't hurt your main domain's reputation. *(Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3p97bZzBHx8)*
- **First actions [Mine]:** (1) Write down the accountability vetting criteria. (2) Build a list of AI automation and implementation freelancers and small agencies in your niche, from the communities where they gather. (3) Send a humble "free, vetted listing, nothing to buy" message. (4) Nurture them with a regular email update on how the directory is going.

### B. Reddit and LinkedIn: useful answers and how-to posts (demand plus AEO)
- **[Nevo]** Reddit gets views without an existing audience. On subs where promotion isn't allowed (e.g., r/SaaS), share how-to posts and real workflows, not product posts. Write down to earth, as one person building the project. *(Source: "Postiz marketing playbook: Reddit launches, n8n templates...", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ul2LaZIPqMY)*
- **[Nevo]** Answer questions people are already asking in your niche with genuinely useful replies. A good Reddit thread or LinkedIn article can become a source that LLMs cite. *(Source: https://x.com/wickedguro/status/2086788813301661865)*
- **[Nevo]** With fewer than about 1k followers, test topics on Reddit first, then repost the winners on LinkedIn and X. Post from the founder's account, not a new company account. *(Sources: "Growing a founder's social following from under 1k followers", https://gitroom.com/blog/social-media-plan-2024; "How Papermark won Product of the Day", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z6TpKJSSrbI)*
- **[Nevo]** Build in public by teaching useful, actionable things. Daily diary updates rarely bring customers. *(Source: Nevo's X posts, Jun and Jul 2026, https://x.com/wickedguro)*
- **First actions [Mine]:** (1) Find 3 to 5 subreddits and LinkedIn groups where small business owners ask "what AI should I use for X." (2) Answer one question a day for a few weeks. (3) Turn the answers that land best into LinkedIn articles, like "Which AI tools a [vertical] business should integrate first, and what to ask a specialist."

### C. A free lead magnet: an "AI fit" guide or quiz, plus featuring specialists
- **[Nevo]** Build a free tool or resource closely tied to your core product. Registering gets users something valuable right away and adds them to your list. *(Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wQmI7zZn1g)*
- **[Nevo]** Publish listicles that feature other people, then tell the people featured so they share them. *(Source: "Launching a Hype-Driven By-Product on Its Own Domain", https://gitroom.com/blog/chatgpt-will-10x-github-project)*
- **[Mine]** Your "helps them determine what types of AI to integrate" feature is a ready-made lead magnet. A short questionnaire that returns a recommended AI stack plus matching specialists brings in founders and sends them to your supply. Featured specialists have a reason to share it.
- **First actions [Mine]:** (1) Write a simple v1 of the "What AI should my business integrate?" questionnaire (it can be a form at first). (2) Publish a "vetted accountable AI specialists for [niche]" roundup and tag the people listed.

**Later, not now:**
- **[Nevo]** SEO pays off only where people already search. Check search volume in Semrush or Ahrefs, target long-tail keywords with roughly 20 to 200 searches, and know that new domains rank slowly (borrowing authority from platforms like Medium helps). *(Source: "Research vs. Browse", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7CAsrkK4ys)*
- **[Nevo]** Product Hunt needs an audience you've built elsewhere. Share one tweet that links to the launch rather than asking for upvotes, and relaunch later around substantial features. *(Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z6TpKJSSrbI; "How Digger Trended on GitHub...", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TwYUJNS2foc; Nevo's X posts, Apr 2026)*
- **[Mine]** Programmatic pages (e.g., "AI specialists for [industry] + [use case]") can come once you have enough real, vetted listings to fill them honestly.

## 4. The 3 most important questions for Justin

1. **Stage and supply:** Is the directory live, and do you already have any specialists (or relationships with any)? How many founders have you talked to?
2. **Niche and your edge:** Which industry or type of business do you know best or have access to, and where do you already have an audience or network (LinkedIn, X, communities, newsletter)?
3. **Business model and what "accountability" means:** How do you plan to make money (specialist listing fees, a per-match or commission fee, paid AI assessments for founders), and what exactly will a specialist have to commit to so it counts as "human accountability"?
